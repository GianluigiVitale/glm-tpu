# D5 / D10 challenger development — 2026-09-19

All bodies are opt-in under `glm_tpu/perf`. Frozen `MODEL_SOURCE` remains
`edecdd94`. These are synthetic-weight/kernel measurements, not real-model
throughput or an admission against DB610.

## D5: local attention and LSE reduce-scatter

`lse_attention_mapped` gathers the head-sharded queries, attends each owner's
selected subset, gathers the tiny FP32 LSE table and reduce-scatters weighted
FP32 outputs over heads. The denominator is calculated from the gathered LSE,
keeping the output payload 512-wide. Empty owners return zero and negative
infinite LSE. Owner health travels with the LSE values and is checked across every owner.

This is **not bitwise equivalent** to the frozen attention: local online maxima
change the BF16 rounding of unnormalized probabilities, and each local output
rounds to BF16 before the FP32 merge. CPU comparisons cover empty selections,
one live token, one active owner, balanced/skewed selections, shuffled physical
pages and duplicate-position refusal. Maximum absolute attention error on the
seeded fixture is 0.001953125; this is an observed fixture bound, not a theorem
for arbitrary weights/activations. Three decode steps on the eight-layer fixture
have the same tokens and selections as frozen, with the existing D8 KV bound
(absolute error <=0.0625 and fewer than 1% differing elements).

The original fused row-DMA primitive did not deliver the estimated speedup.
It reads an aligned eight-row group for every selected key. Replacing that with
an XLA local gather and contiguous Pallas tiles improved the measured cost:

| Selected rows | frozen selected-KV exchange | fused row-DMA LSE | gathered-tile LSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 64-token prefix | 122–123 us | 120 us | 110–111 us |
| 2,048 balanced | 123–124 us | 126 us | 111–112 us |
| 2,048 on one owner | 123–124 us | 228–229 us | 118–119 us |

[Eight-rank receipt](tpu-microbench-d5-gathered-20260919T135152Z.json),
32 TPU v4 chips, real attention geometry, capacity 131,072, 40 timed calls.
Reported per-body slopes compare chains of one and 17 query-dependent calls;
cache and selection are fixed within each chain. Full-step timing is needed to
account for compiler movement of invariant work. The receipt predates the
512-wide scatter change. The
[first fused-only receipt](tpu-microbench-d5-20260919T134755Z.json) is retained.

## D10: score physical pages and check shortlist coverage

`score_cache_pages` scores the physical index-cache rows directly, then gathers
one scalar score per key into logical order. It avoids gathering the full
128-component key vector and preserves ascending logical positions for a
sort-free local top-k.

`two_stage_topk_mapped` selects L+1 pairs per owner (L=512 by default). The first
L enter the global merge. The extra pair is checked against the global Kth
score **and position**, so equal-score ties preserve lowest-position ordering.
Any omitted pair that can win causes a replicated full-K fallback. Nonfinite
scores also use the frozen selector. A short union cannot be accepted silently.

CPU proofs compare all positions, counts and FP32 scores bitwise against the
frozen path: 60 randomized trials with ties and skew, both cut-check branches,
physical-page permutation, and causal multirow prefill selection. Combined
D5+D10 passes the decoder fixture with the same token/selection checks as D5.

## Prefill primitives

The gathered-tile LSE kernel also accepts multirow queries. CPU checks cover
one and three rows. `prefill_dsa_one_pass_mapped` replaces the repeated
512-key score/top-k/merge chain with one full local score row and one shortlist,
preserving the causal bounds and finite-score health checks. Its per-head
intermediate is **64 MiB** at 32 rows and 128K capacity (the final summed score
row is 2 MiB). CPU comparisons with the frozen tiled selector are bitwise equal
for positions, scores and health, including empty and partial causal prefixes.
These primitives are not yet wired into the full prefill runtime.
